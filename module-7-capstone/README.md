# Module 7 · Final Exercise & Assessment

**Estimated time:** ~5–7 hrs  ·  **Topics:** 5

This is where the course stops teaching and starts testing. You will build one production-quality application that uses every pattern from Modules 1–6, score it against a published rubric, optionally submit it for verification, and then plan your route to **ServiceNow Software Architect**.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- Build a layered, production-quality scoped application end to end
- Apply dependency injection, secure coding, integration, and global error handling together in one build
- Grade your own work objectively against a 100-point professional rubric
- Explain and defend your architectural decisions in an interview
- Plan your certification path and your progression to AI-ready architect

## Prerequisites

All previous modules (0–6). Do not start the final exercise until you have completed the hands-on tasks in Modules 2–6 — this exercise assumes all of them.

## How this module works

1. **Read the [rubric](01-scoring-rubric.md) first.** Know how you will be graded before you build — that is how professionals work.
2. **Build the [final exercise](00-final-exercise.md)** in your `x_course_lab` scoped application.
3. **Score yourself** honestly against the rubric. Anything below 70 means go back to the module that covers the gap.
4. **Optionally [submit for verification](02-submit-for-verification.md)** as an [Exam Navigator member](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCMf7pje1x5iZkkEF-Y3PLSA/join) to get reviewed feedback.
5. **Plan what's next** with the [architect roadmap](03-architect-roadmap.md) and [certification path](04-certification-and-career.md).

## Knowledge check

Before you call yourself done, make sure you can answer these:

- Does your build keep business logic out of Business Rules? Where does it live instead, and why?
- Is every external call isolated so a third-party outage cannot corrupt your data?
- Could someone swap your integration for a fake one to test your service? If not, why not?
- Where does an unexpected error surface, and would you find it a week later?
- What did you score on the rubric, and what is your plan for the lowest-scoring criterion?
